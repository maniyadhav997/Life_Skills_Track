# Tiny Habits

#### Question 1

###### In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

BJ Fogg explains that we should not depend on motivation to build habits. Instead, we should make new habits very small and easy.

He suggests three things:

1\. **Start Small:** Begin with an easy task, like doing 2 push-ups instead of 50.

2\. **Use Existing Habits:** Connect a new habit to something you already do. For example, do 2 push-ups after brushing your teeth.

3\. **Celebrate:** After completing the task, say "Awesome!" to make yourself feel happy and motivated.


#### Question 2

###### How can you use B = MAP to make making new habits easier? What are M, A and P.

BJ Fogg's B = MAP model explains that three things help us build a new habit:

1. **Motivation:** Don't depend on motivation or willpower. Make the habit easy so you can do it even when you don't feel motivated.

2. **Ability:** Make the habit very small. For example, instead of studying for one hour, start with just 5 minutes.

3. **Prompt:** Connect your new habit to something you already do every day. For example, after brushing your teeth, read one page.

**Shine (Celebration):** After completing the habit, celebrate yourself by saying "Awesome!" This makes you feel good and encourages you to repeat it.

###### Why it is important to "Shine" or Celebrate after each successful completion of habit? (This is the most important concept in today's topic. Whatever you celebrate becomes a habit)

Shining means celebrating yourself immediately after completing a habit.

It is important because:
- It makes your brain feel happy about completing the task.
- It creates a positive feeling towards the habit.
- It encourages you to repeat the same behavior.
- It builds confidence and makes the habit easier to continue.
Example: After completing 2 push-ups, say "Awesome! I did it!"



#### Question 4

###### In this video, what was the most interesting story or idea for you?

The most interesting idea shared by James Clear is 1% improvement every day, also called Atomic Habits or the aggregation of marginal gains.

He explains this using the example of British Cycling and Dave Brailsford.
- **The Concept:** Instead of making big changes, focus on improving small things by just 1% every day.
- **The Example:** The British Cycling team improved small things like bike seats, sleeping habits, and hygiene.
- **The Result:** These small improvements added up over time and helped the team achieve great success, including winning many Olympic gold medals.


#### Question 5

###### What is the book's perspective about Identity?

James Clear explains that changing your identity is the best way to build long-lasting habits.

He says there are three levels of behavior change:
1. **Outcomes:** What you want to achieve. Example: Lose weight.
2. **Processes:** What you do to achieve it. Example: Going to the gym regularly.
3. **Identity:** How you see yourself. Example: Believing that you are a healthy person.
Instead of saying, "I want to lose weight," start believing, "I am a healthy person."

When you believe in a new identity, your daily actions will slowly match that belief.

#### Question 6

###### Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit easier to do?

James Clear explains that the third law of behavior change is "Make It Easy."

The main idea is to make good habits easier to do and bad habits harder to do.

Three important strategies:

1. **Prepare Your Environment:** Keep things ready so you can easily start a good habit. Example: Keep your guitar near your desk so you remember to practice.

2. **Reduce Effort:** Remove unnecessary steps that make starting a habit difficult.

3. **Design Your Environment:** Make good habits easier to access and bad habits harder to access. Example: Keep your phone away while studying.


#### Question 7

###### Write about the book's perspective on how to make a habit harder to do?

James Clear explains that to break bad habits, we should use the opposite of the four laws of behavior change.

1. **Make It Invisible:** Remove things that remind you of bad habits. Example: Keep your phone away while studying.

2. **Make It Unattractive:** Focus on the negative effects of bad habits. Example: Remember how scrolling too much wastes your time.

3. **Make It Difficult:** Add extra steps to make bad habits harder. Example: Log out of social media apps.

4. **Make It Unsatisfying:** Create a consequence for bad habits. Example: Ask a friend to hold you accountable when you waste time.
